# Flashcards

## Card 1

Front:

What is law?

Back:

A system of rules and principles established by governmental authority to regulate behaviour within society.

---

## Card 2

Front:

Four key characteristics of law

Back:

Binding nature (enforceable rights and obligations); systematic structure; social function (collective needs, order); normative framework (standards of conduct).

---

## Card 3

Front:

Four ways law differs from other rules

Back:

Source and authority (government); enforcement (formal sanctions); scope (universal in a country); formal recognition (codified).

---

## Card 4

Front:

Three ways legal rules are created

Back:

Legislative (statutes by elected bodies, regulations by agencies); judicial (case law, precedent, common law); international (treaties, customary international law).

---

## Card 5

Front:

The five roles of law

Back:

Regulatory, protective, facilitative, dispute resolution, educational.

---

## Card 6

Front:

Six reasons people obey the law

Back:

Fear of punishment; social contract; mutual benefit; legitimacy of government; moral or religious duty; socialisation from childhood.

---

## Card 7

Front:

What is the 'social contract' reason for obeying law?

Back:

People give up some personal freedoms in exchange for the protection and order that laws provide.

---

## Card 8

Front:

Lecture definition of data protection

Back:

Safeguarding personal and sensitive information from unauthorised access, use, disclosure or destruction.

---

## Card 9

Front:

Six reasons data protection is important

Back:

Protecting privacy; customer trust; mitigating financial risk; business reputation; preventing cyber threats; legal compliance.

---

## Card 10

Front:

Which UDHR article protects private and family life, and when?

Back:

Article 12, UDHR 1948.

---

## Card 11

Front:

Does the UN framework recognise data protection as a fundamental right?

Back:

No. It protects privacy (UDHR Art. 12, ICCPR), not personal data protection as a separate fundamental right.

---

## Card 12

Front:

ECHR: dates, article, court

Back:

Adopted 1950, in force 1953. Article 8 (private life) covers data protection. European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg, set up 1959.

---

## Card 13

Front:

What is Convention 108?

Back:

Council of Europe Convention for the protection of individuals with regard to automatic processing of personal data (1980 in the lecture).

---

## Card 14

Front:

EU Charter of Fundamental Rights: dates and data protection article

Back:

Adopted 2000; legally binding primary law from 1 December 2009. Article 8 = protection of personal data.

---

## Card 15

Front:

Directive 95/46/EC

Back:

The EU's main data protection instrument from 1995 until May 2018, replaced by the GDPR because it was ineffective.

---

## Card 16

Front:

GDPR key dates

Back:

Adopted April 2016; fully applicable 25 May 2018.

---

## Card 17

Front:

New privacy concepts born from the information society

Back:

'Informational privacy' and the 'right to informational self-determination'.

---

## Card 18

Front:

When did EU data protection law begin, and why?

Back:

1970s: some states adopted laws to control processing by public authorities and large companies.
