# Learning Objectives

Master Chapter 5 — Specific types of data under the GDPR — well enough to answer both multiple-choice and written exam questions on it.

# Lesson

> **In one sentence:** Some data is so personal that misuse can lead to discrimination or serious harm, so the GDPR starts from "you may not process it" and only allows it in a short list of situations, with extra safeguards. Criminal records get their own strict rule.

## Special categories of personal data `Art. 9(1)`

Special categories are personal data considered more **sensitive** and requiring **higher protection**, because processing them can significantly affect **fundamental rights and freedoms**. Art. 9(1) **prohibits** processing them, unless an exception applies.

| Category                              | Meaning                                                  | Lecture example                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Racial or ethnic origin               | Reveals race, ancestry, cultural background              | A school cannot use students' ethnicity for placement decisions                         |
| Political opinions                    | Political views or affiliations                          | A party cannot use voters' opinions for unsolicited targeted ads                        |
| Religious or philosophical beliefs    | Religious faith or philosophical convictions             | A charity cannot process donors' religion without an exception                          |
| Trade union membership                | Whether someone is a union member                        | An employer cannot collect it without explicit consent (unless labour law requires)     |
| Genetic data                          | An individual's genetic makeup                           | A research institute needs explicit consent from study participants                     |
| Biometric data (to uniquely identify) | Fingerprints, facial recognition, voice                  | A school using fingerprint scanning for students needs explicit consent                 |
| Health data                           | Physical or mental health, records, diagnoses, treatment | An insurer cannot use health data for coverage decisions without an exception           |
| Sex life or sexual orientation        | Sexual preferences, practices, orientation               | A dating site cannot share orientation data with third parties without explicit consent |

> **🧠 Memory hook: group them**
>
> **Beliefs** (political, religious/philosophical) + **Origin** (racial/ethnic) + **Body** (genetic, biometric, health, sex life/orientation) + **Membership** (trade union) = 8 categories.

## Exceptions (lecture's six)

| Exception                                                                                          | Lecture example                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Explicit consent** (explain purpose, data, risks and benefits; right to withdraw at any time) | Medical research organisation studying genetic predisposition to disease                                                               |
| **Necessary to comply with EU or Member State law**                                             | Police collect racial data in a racially-motivated crime investigation; Social Services collect family data for child protection       |
| **Vital interests** of the data subject or another person                                       | Social worker collects parents' religious beliefs for a child at risk of abuse; hospital uses genetic data to protect a patient's life |
| **Archiving in the public interest, scientific or historical research, statistics**             | Research on political opinions and ethnicity using **anonymised** data; historical study of religious figures                       |
| **Establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims**                                          | Court collects data on a client's ethnicity to establish racial discrimination; employee uses own trade union data in a dispute        |
| **Substantial public interest** based on EU or Member State law with appropriate safeguards     | Government agency collects ethnicity data to run a racial-equality programme; research on rare disease genetics                        |

> **📚 Beyond the slides**
>
> Art. 9(2) of the GDPR actually lists ten exceptions, including employment and social security law, not-for-profit bodies, data manifestly made public by the person, preventive or occupational medicine, and public health. For the exam, the lecture's six are the priority.

## Additional safeguards for special categories

- **Higher threshold for lawful processing**: the controller needs a lawful basis under `Art. 6` **and** a separate condition under `Art. 9`.
- **Additional transparency**: tell people the **purpose**, the **categories** of data and the **recipients**.
- **Additional security**: encryption, access controls, physical security.
- **Additional rights**: object, erasure, restriction; extra restrictions exist for genetic and biometric data.
- Specific measures: **pseudonymisation or anonymisation**; **encryption** (readable only with a decryption key); **access controls** (authorised personnel only); **physical security** (locked data centres); **breach notification** to the authority and affected people; **DPIA**; **Data Protection Officer** to oversee compliance.

## Criminal convictions and offences `Art. 10`

Processing is only permitted under specific conditions: either **under the control of an official authority**, or **authorised by EU or Member State law with appropriate safeguards**.

- Any **comprehensive register of criminal convictions** must be kept **solely under the control of an official authority**. Private companies cannot keep one.
- Appropriate technical and organisational **security** measures.
- **Minimum** necessary data, for the specific purpose; **accurate and up to date**; kept **no longer than necessary**.

### Purposes allowed

- **Criminal justice**: prevention, detection, investigation, prosecution and punishment of offences, execution of judgments.
- **Public safety**: national security, preventing terrorism, threats to public health.
- **Employment**: checking criminal records for jobs with vulnerable people or requiring security clearance.

### Restrictions and safeguards

- People can **access** their criminal data and contest its accuracy and completeness.
- No **disclosure** to unauthorised third parties without consent or a legal basis.
- Right to **erasure** when no longer necessary or unlawfully obtained.
- Controllers must **document** processing and inform people how their data is used.

> **⚖️ Examples**
>
> **Background checks**: a care company hiring staff for children or elderly people may check criminal records, using only the minimum data and **erasing records once the hiring decision is made**.
**Police investigations and counter-terrorism**: permitted, but still bound by minimisation, purpose limitation, onward-disclosure limits, and access and rectification rights.

> **⚠️ Exam traps**
>
> - Criminal data is **not** an Art. 9 special category; it is Art. 10.
>
> - Biometric data is special only when processed **to uniquely identify** a person.
>
> - Having an Art. 9 exception is not enough; you still need an Art. 6 lawful basis.
