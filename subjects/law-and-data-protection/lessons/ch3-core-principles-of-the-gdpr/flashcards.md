# Flashcards

## Card 1

Front:

The seven principles of Art. 5

Back:

Lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; accountability.

---

## Card 2

Front:

Maximum fine for breaching the basic principles

Back:

Art. 83(5)(a): up to €20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

---

## Card 3

Front:

The six lawful bases in Art. 6(1)

Back:

(a) consent (b) contract &lpar;c&rpar; legal obligation (d) vital interests (e) public task (f) legitimate interests.

---

## Card 4

Front:

Legal definition of consent

Back:

Any freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of wishes, by a statement or clear affirmative action, signifying agreement.

---

## Card 5

Front:

What never counts as consent?

Back:

Silence, pre-ticked boxes, inactivity. Consent cannot be implied.

---

## Card 6

Front:

What must 'specific' consent tell the person?

Back:

Controller's identity, what data, how it will be used, the purpose; it must refer to clearly identified processing activities.

---

## Card 7

Front:

What must 'informed' consent include?

Back:

Right to withdraw at any time (as easy as giving it); use for automated decision-making; risks of transfers without adequacy or safeguards.

---

## Card 8

Front:

When does 'freely given' fail?

Back:

When there is inappropriate pressure or influence; the person has no real choice.

---

## Card 9

Front:

Consent inside terms of service

Back:

Allowed if in clear, plain, intelligible, accessible language. Any part violating the GDPR is not binding.

---

## Card 10

Front:

Vital interests basis is reserved for...

Back:

Genuine emergencies where someone's life is at risk.

---

## Card 11

Front:

Four key elements of the public task basis

Back:

Public interest or official authority; task established by law; processing necessary; usually public authorities.

---

## Card 12

Front:

Six results that make processing unlawful (besides no lawful basis)

Back:

Breach of confidence; exceeding legal powers; copyright infringement; breach of contract; breach of industry-specific regulation; breach of the ECHR.

---

## Card 13

Front:

Three elements of fairness

Back:

Reasonable expectations; no unjustified adverse effects; informing data subjects of risks.

---

## Card 14

Front:

Transparency: when must information be given?

Back:

Before processing, and accessible during processing; clear, easy to understand (Arts. 13 and 14).

---

## Card 15

Front:

Purpose limitation, Art. 5(1)(b)

Back:

Collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes; not further processed in a way incompatible with them.

---

## Card 16

Front:

Purposes automatically treated as compatible

Back:

Archiving in the public interest; scientific or historical research; statistics (Art. 89(1)).

---

## Card 17

Front:

Three ways a new purpose becomes acceptable

Back:

It is compatible; specific consent for it; a clear legal provision requiring or allowing it in the public interest.

---

## Card 18

Front:

Five compatibility factors

Back:

Link between purposes; context and reasonable expectations; nature of the data; consequences; safeguards (encryption, pseudonymisation).

---

## Card 19

Front:

Data minimisation, Art. 5(1)&lpar;c&rpar;

Back:

Adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the purposes.

---

## Card 20

Front:

Purpose limitation vs data minimisation

Back:

Purpose limitation = WHY (only for the stated purpose). Minimisation = HOW MUCH (only the data needed for it).

---

## Card 21

Front:

Digital Rights Ireland (2014)

Back:

CJEU C-293/12 and C-594/12, 8 April 2014. Data Retention Directive covered all people, all communications, all traffic data without differentiation; retention of 6 to 24 months had no objective criteria. Disproportionate.

---

## Card 22

Front:

Accuracy, Art. 5(1)(d)

Back:

Accurate and, where necessary, up to date; inaccurate data erased or rectified without delay. Inaccurate = incorrect or misleading as to any matter of fact.

---

## Card 23

Front:

Rijkeboer case

Back:

CJEU C-553/07 (2009). Right to privacy means certainty data is processed correctly and lawfully; right of access lets people check their data is accurate, including who received it.

---

## Card 24

Front:

When is updating data prohibited?

Back:

When records document events as a historical snapshot (e.g. operation records). Only additions clearly marked as later.

---

## Card 25

Front:

Storage limitation, Art. 5(1)(e)

Back:

Identifiable form no longer than necessary; longer only solely for archiving, research or statistics with safeguards.

---

## Card 26

Front:

S. and Marper v UK

Back:

ECtHR: indefinite retention of fingerprints, cell samples and DNA of people acquitted or whose case was discontinued was disproportionate.

---

## Card 27

Front:

Three conditions for exceptions to storage limitation

Back:

Provided by law; respects the essence of fundamental rights; necessary and proportionate for a legitimate aim.

---

## Card 28

Front:

Integrity and confidentiality, Art. 5(1)(f)

Back:

Appropriate security against unauthorised or unlawful processing and accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organisational measures.

---

## Card 29

Front:

Art. 32 factors for choosing security measures

Back:

State of the art; costs of implementation; nature, scope, context and purposes; risk (likelihood and severity).

---

## Card 30

Front:

Two security measures named by the GDPR

Back:

Pseudonymisation and encryption.

---

## Card 31

Front:

Accountability, Art. 5(2)

Back:

Controller is responsible for AND able to demonstrate compliance with the principles.

---

## Card 32

Front:

Art. 24(1) controller duty

Back:

Implement risk-based, proportionate technical and organisational measures to ensure and demonstrate compliance; review and update them.

---

## Card 33

Front:

Processor duties linked to accountability

Back:

Keep records of processing operations; appoint a Data Protection Officer.
