# Exercises

## Chapter 3 MCQ

### Question 1

How many key principles does Article 5 GDPR set out?

- A. Five
- B. Six
- C. Seven
- D. Eight

Answer: C. Seven. Seven: lawfulness/fairness/transparency, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, accountability.

---

### Question 2

Infringing the basic principles can lead to a fine of up to:

- A. €10 million or 2% of turnover, whichever is lower
- B. €20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher
- C. €20 million or 4%, whichever is lower
- D. €1 million flat

Answer: B. €20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Art. 83(5)(a), the highest tier: €20m or 4%, whichever is higher.

---

### Question 3

A website shows a pre-ticked box saying 'I agree to marketing emails'. This is:

- A. Valid consent because the user can untick it
- B. Invalid, because consent must be an unambiguous clear affirmative act
- C. Valid if in the privacy policy
- D. Valid for existing customers only

Answer: B. Invalid, because consent must be an unambiguous clear affirmative act. Silence, pre-ticked boxes and inactivity do not constitute consent.

---

### Question 4

A checkout page asks for one consent covering marketing, non-EU transfers and sharing with partners. Which consent element fails?

- A. Freely given
- B. Specific
- C. Unambiguous
- D. Withdrawable

Answer: B. Specific. Bundling several purposes into one consent fails the 'specific' element.

---

### Question 5

A payroll department reports employee salaries monthly to the tax authority. The lawful basis is:

- A. Consent
- B. Legitimate interests
- C. Legal obligation
- D. Vital interests

Answer: C. Legal obligation. Processing is required by tax law: Art. 6(1)&lpar;c&rpar;.

---

### Question 6

A man collapses unconscious; paramedics check his wallet and find an allergy card. The lawful basis is:

- A. Vital interests
- B. Public task
- C. Contract
- D. Consent

Answer: A. Vital interests. Vital interests is reserved for genuine emergencies where life is at risk.

---

### Question 7

A company installs CCTV in its car park and hallways to prevent theft. The most likely lawful basis is:

- A. Public task
- B. Legitimate interests
- C. Legal obligation
- D. Vital interests

Answer: B. Legitimate interests. Preventing theft and ensuring safety is a legitimate interest of the controller.

---

### Question 8

A telecom company keeps call logs for 5 years although industry rules allow 6 months. Besides storage limitation, this is an example of unlawfulness through:

- A. Breach of a duty of confidence
- B. Breach of industry-specific legislation
- C. Copyright infringement
- D. Exceeding legal powers

Answer: B. Breach of industry-specific legislation. The MobileTel example: breach of industry-specific regulation.

---

### Question 9

A flashlight app collects and sells users' contact lists. This mainly violates:

- A. Accuracy
- B. Fairness (reasonable expectations)
- C. Storage limitation only
- D. Portability

Answer: B. Fairness (reasonable expectations). No reasonable connection to the app's function: it violates reasonable expectations.

---

### Question 10

A GP gives his patient list to his wife's travel agency for holiday offers. Which principle is breached?

- A. Accuracy
- B. Purpose limitation
- C. Portability
- D. Accountability only

Answer: B. Purpose limitation. The new purpose is incompatible with the original medical purpose.

---

### Question 11

An employer keeps blood group data for ALL employees, though only hazardous-work staff need it. This breaches:

- A. Data minimisation
- B. Accuracy
- C. Transparency
- D. The right to object

Answer: A. Data minimisation. Holding it for other staff is irrelevant and excessive.

---

### Question 12

Which further purposes are NOT considered incompatible with the original purpose?

- A. Direct marketing
- B. Archiving in the public interest, scientific or historical research, statistics
- C. Selling to data brokers
- D. Profiling for advertising

Answer: B. Archiving in the public interest, scientific or historical research, statistics. Art. 5(1)(b) with Art. 89(1).

---

### Question 13

S. and Marper v UK is mainly associated with which principle?

- A. Storage limitation
- B. Consent
- C. Portability
- D. Transparency

Answer: A. Storage limitation. Indefinite retention of DNA and fingerprints of people not convicted was disproportionate.

---

### Question 14

Which is NOT a factor under Art. 32 for choosing security measures?

- A. State of the art
- B. Costs of implementation
- C. Nature, scope, context and purposes of processing
- D. The controller's annual profit target

Answer: D. The controller's annual profit target. Art. 32(1): state of the art, costs, nature/scope/context/purposes, and risk.

---

### Question 15

Accountability under Art. 5(2) requires the controller to:

- A. Only appoint a DPO
- B. Be responsible for and be able to demonstrate compliance
- C. Obtain consent for all processing
- D. Notify every processing activity to the SA

Answer: B. Be responsible for and be able to demonstrate compliance. Two parts: responsibility and the ability to demonstrate compliance.

---

### Question 16

The Rijkeboer case links the accuracy principle with:

- A. The right of access, to check data is correct
- B. The right to portability
- C. Direct marketing
- D. Automated decisions

Answer: A. The right of access, to check data is correct. The CJEU said people need access to check that their data is accurate and lawfully disclosed.

---

### Question 17

A medical record of an operation later turns out to contain wrong findings. The controller should:

- A. Overwrite the record
- B. Delete the record
- C. Keep the record and add clearly marked later remarks
- D. Transfer it to another hospital

Answer: C. Keep the record and add clearly marked later remarks. Historical snapshot records must not be 'updated'; only marked additions are allowed.

---

### Question 18

A marketing firm buys email addresses that a broker hacked from a competitor. Its marketing purpose is legitimate. The processing is:

- A. Lawful because the purpose is legitimate
- B. Unlawful because the data was obtained unlawfully
- C. Lawful if the firm didn't know
- D. Lawful under vital interests

Answer: B. Unlawful because the data was obtained unlawfully. Using unlawfully obtained data is unlawful even with a legitimate purpose.
