# Exercises

## Chapter 2 MCQ

### Question 1

Personal data is any information relating to:

- A. Any legal or natural person
- B. An identified or identifiable natural person
- C. Only sensitive information about a person
- D. Only information stored electronically

Answer: B. An identified or identifiable natural person. The definition covers identified or identifiable natural persons, in any form.

---

### Question 2

Which of the following is NOT personal data under the GDPR?

- A. An IP address
- B. Pseudonymised patient records
- C. Information about a deceased person
- D. A fingerprint scan

Answer: C. Information about a deceased person. The GDPR does not apply to deceased persons. IP addresses, pseudonymised data and fingerprints are personal data.

---

### Question 3

Pseudonymised data:

- A. Is outside the GDPR
- B. Is still personal data and subject to the GDPR
- C. Becomes anonymous after one year
- D. Is a special category

Answer: B. Is still personal data and subject to the GDPR. Because it can be re-identified with extra information, pseudonymised data remains personal data.

---

### Question 4

Previously anonymised data becomes personal data again when:

- A. It is stored for more than five years
- B. The data subject provides additional information enabling identification to exercise their rights
- C. It is transferred outside the EEA
- D. The controller encrypts it

Answer: B. The data subject provides additional information enabling identification to exercise their rights. If the person provides extra information that allows identification, the data becomes personal again.

---

### Question 5

Which three factors decide whether data 'relates to' an individual?

- A. Content, purpose, result or effect
- B. Format, size, location
- C. Consent, contract, legal obligation
- D. Accuracy, storage, security

Answer: A. Content, purpose, result or effect. Content (about them?), purpose (used to evaluate them?), and results or effects on them.

---

### Question 6

Processing of data about criminal convictions is governed by:

- A. Article 9, as a special category
- B. Article 10, under official authority control or authorised by law with safeguards
- C. Article 6 only
- D. It is not regulated

Answer: B. Article 10, under official authority control or authorised by law with safeguards. Criminal data is separate from Art. 9 and governed by Art. 10.

---

### Question 7

Which of these is an Art. 9 special category?

- A. Salary
- B. Home address
- C. Trade union membership
- D. Email address

Answer: C. Trade union membership. Trade union membership is listed in Art. 9. The others are ordinary personal data.

---

### Question 8

Presenting a special chip card to access a bank safe is authentication by:

- A. Something you know
- B. Something you are
- C. A token you possess
- D. Pseudonymisation

Answer: C. A token you possess. A token exclusively in the person's possession (chip card, key).

---

### Question 9

To decide whether a person is identifiable, you consider:

- A. Only the name
- B. All reasonable means likely to be used to identify them, directly or indirectly
- C. Only the data held by the controller today
- D. Whether the person has consented

Answer: B. All reasonable means likely to be used to identify them, directly or indirectly. All reasonable means likely to be used, e.g. singling out.

---

### Question 10

Trade secrets and acquisition plans are best described as:

- A. Special category data
- B. Sensitive business information
- C. Biometric data
- D. Pseudonymised data

Answer: B. Sensitive business information. They are sensitive business information, a risk to the company, not personal data by nature.
