# Flashcards

## Card 1

Front:

Definition of personal data

Back:

Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.

---

## Card 2

Front:

Identified vs identifiable

Back:

Identified: already distinguished from everyone else. Identifiable: can be identified directly or indirectly, using all reasonable means likely to be used (e.g. singling out).

---

## Card 3

Front:

Examples of identifiers

Back:

Name, ID number, location data, online identifiers (email, IP address, device ID, screen name), genetic data, biometric data.

---

## Card 4

Front:

Three factors deciding whether data 'relates to' a person

Back:

Content (is it about them?), purpose (used to evaluate or treat them?), result or effect (does it impact them?).

---

## Card 5

Front:

Forms personal data can take

Back:

Written or spoken, images, CCTV footage and sound, electronic and paper records, cell samples containing DNA.

---

## Card 6

Front:

Anonymisation

Back:

Removing identifying information so the person can no longer be identified. The data is no longer personal data and the GDPR no longer applies.

---

## Card 7

Front:

When can anonymised data become personal data again?

Back:

When the data subject provides extra information, to exercise access, rectification, erasure, restriction or portability rights, that makes identification possible.

---

## Card 8

Front:

Pseudonymisation

Back:

Replacing identifiers (codes, encryption) so re-identification is possible with extra information. It is STILL personal data and the GDPR applies.

---

## Card 9

Front:

Authentication: three methods

Back:

Something you are (biometrics, e.g. passport photo, fingerprint); something you know (PIN, password); something you have (chip card, key). Electronic signatures too.

---

## Card 10

Front:

The Art. 9 special categories

Back:

Racial or ethnic origin; political opinions; religious or philosophical beliefs; trade union membership; genetic data; biometric data to identify; health; sex life or sexual orientation.

---

## Card 11

Front:

Criminal conviction data: which article and rule?

Back:

Art. 10. Only under the control of official authority, or when authorised by EU or Member State law with appropriate safeguards.

---

## Card 12

Front:

Three things that are NOT personal data

Back:

Truly anonymised data; data about deceased persons; data about companies or public authorities.

---

## Card 13

Front:

Is sensitive business information personal data?

Back:

Not by nature. Trade secrets, acquisition plans, financial data are a risk to the company, not personal data categories.

---

## Card 14

Front:

How does the GDPR achieve data protection?

Back:

By conferring rights on individuals and imposing responsibilities on those who process personal data.
