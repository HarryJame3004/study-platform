# Learning Objectives

Master Chapter 2 — The concept of personal data — well enough to answer both multiple-choice and written exam questions on it.

# Lesson

> **In one sentence:** Personal data is any information that points to a living person, either by itself or when combined with other information. If you can truly never link it back to anyone, it stops being personal data and the GDPR no longer applies.

## Definition

**Personal data = any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.** It can be obtained **directly** from the information processed, or **indirectly** by combining it with other information. It may include special categories of data and criminal conviction data, which are more sensitive and processed only in limited circumstances.

## Identified vs identifiable

- **Identified**: the person is already distinguished from all others and recognisable as an individual (e.g. "Nguyen Van A, student ID 2021001").
- **Identifiable**: the person can be identified, directly or indirectly. To decide, take into account **all reasonable means likely to be used** to identify the individual, for example **singling out** (making it possible to treat one person differently from others).

Identification requires elements that describe a person so that they are **distinguishable** from all other persons and **recognisable** as an individual.

## Common identifiers (non-exhaustive)

- Name
- Identification number (e.g. social security number)
- Location data (e.g. home address)
- Online identifier (e-mail, screen name, IP address, device ID)
- Genetic data (e.g. biological samples)
- Biometric data (fingerprints, facial recognition)

## What does "relates to" mean?

Information must relate to the individual. Three factors decide this:

- **Content**: is the data directly about the person or their activities?
- **Purpose**: is the data processed to evaluate, treat or influence that person?
- **Result or effect**: does processing have an impact on the person?

## Forms of personal data

Written or spoken communications and images; CCTV footage or sound; electronic records and paper records; cell samples of human tissue (they record DNA, i.e. inherited or acquired genetic characteristics). The form does not matter: all can be personal data.

## Sensitive data

Personal data that can cause harm or embarrassment: racial or ethnic origin, political or religious beliefs, genetic and biometric data, health, sexual orientation. Do not confuse this with **sensitive business information** (trade secrets, acquisition plans, financial data, supplier and customer information), which is a risk to the company, not a category of personal data.

## Data protection under the GDPR

Data protection = safeguarding the privacy rights of individuals concerning the processing of personal data. The GDPR does this by **conferring rights on individuals** and **imposing responsibilities on those who process** personal data.

## Anonymisation vs pseudonymisation

|                      | Anonymisation                                                              | Pseudonymisation                                                                                                   |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What it is           | Removing identifying information so the person can no longer be identified | Replacing identifiers with codes or encrypting them, so re-identification is still possible with extra information |
| Still personal data? | **No**                                                                  | **Yes**                                                                                                         |
| GDPR applies?        | No                                                                         | Yes                                                                                                                |

Storage limitation requires data to be kept in identifiable form only as long as necessary. If a controller wants to keep data after it has served its purpose, the data must be **erased or anonymised**.

> **⚠️ Exam trap: anonymised data can become personal data again**
>
> If the data subject, to exercise rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction or portability, **provides additional information that enables identification**, previously anonymised data become personal data again.

## Authentication

A procedure by which a person proves they **possess a certain identity** and/or are **authorised** to do certain things (enter a security area, withdraw money). Methods:

- Comparing **biometric data** (photo or fingerprints in a passport at immigration control): something you are.
- Asking for information only that person should know (**PIN, password**): something you know.
- Requiring a **token** only that person should possess (chip card, key to a bank safe): something you have.
- **Electronic signatures**, sometimes with PINs, identify and authenticate people in electronic communications.

## Special categories `Art. 9`

- Racial or ethnic origin
- Political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs
- Trade union membership
- Genetic data, and biometric data processed to identify a person
- Health, sex life or sexual orientation

## Criminal convictions and offences `Art. 10`

Not in the Art. 9 list; they have **separate rules**. Processing may only be carried out "under the control of official authority or when authorised by Union or Member State law providing for appropriate safeguards". Such data can only be kept under the control of specific official authorities.

## What is NOT personal data?

- Truly anonymised data (pseudonymised data **is** still personal data).
- Information about a **deceased person**.
- Information about **companies or public authorities** (legal persons, not natural persons).

> **🧠 Quick test for any piece of information**
>
> 1) Is it about a living natural person? 2) Can that person be identified, by any reasonable means, alone or combined with other data? If both are yes, it is personal data.
