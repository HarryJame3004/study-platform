# Learning Objectives

Master Chapter 6 — Remedies, liability, penalties and compensation — well enough to answer both multiple-choice and written exam questions on it.

# Lesson

> **In one sentence:** If your data rights are broken you can complain to the data protection authority, go to court, let a non-profit fight for you, and claim money for your losses. Separately, the authority can fine the company up to €20 million or 4% of global turnover.

## Right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority

- Every data subject may complain to a supervisory authority (SA). SAs must make complaining easy, e.g. an **electronic complaint form**.
- **Where?** The Member State of the person's **habitual residence**, **place of work**, or **place of the alleged infringement**.
- Complaints **must be investigated**, and the SA must **inform the person of the outcome**.
- Data subjects, controllers and processors can **appeal to the courts** against SA decisions.

## Right to an effective judicial remedy

- A person whose rights are violated can bring a claim before a court. Choosing SA or court is the data subject's option.
- **Where?** The Member State where the **controller or processor has an establishment**, or where the data subject has **habitual residence**.
- If the controller or processor is a **public authority exercising public powers**, only the courts of **that authority's State** are competent.
- If **EU institutions or bodies** process data unlawfully, complaints go to the **General Court of the EU** (part of the CJEU).

### Preliminary rulings

Data subjects, SAs, controllers or processors can ask the **national court** to request clarification from the **CJEU** on the **interpretation and validity** of acts of EU institutions. The CJEU **must answer** and cannot refuse on the ground that the answer is not relevant or timely for the original case. It **can refuse only if the question is outside its competence**.

## Right to mandate a not-for-profit body

A person can mandate a non-profit organisation to lodge a complaint or go to court on their behalf. The body must have **statutory objectives in the public interest** and be **active in data protection**. It may bring **collective claims** for many data subjects. Depending on Member State law, a body may act **without being mandated**.

## Liability and the right to compensation

Anyone who suffers damage from processing that infringes the law can claim compensation for:

| Material damage                                                                                                                                                | Non-material damage                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Out of pocket" losses, objectively quantifiable. Usually **secondary harm** caused indirectly: losing a job, being denied a contract, price discrimination | The **emotional** damage caused by the illegal processing itself (distress, anxiety, humiliation) |

The concept of damage should be **interpreted broadly** in light of CJEU case law and the aims of the GDPR.

### Who pays?

- **Controllers** are liable if they do not meet their GDPR obligations.
- **Processors** are liable **only** where they breached obligations **specifically addressed to processors**, or acted **outside or contrary to the controller's lawful instructions**.
- Where several controllers or processors caused the damage, **each is liable for the entire damage**, so the victim gets **full and effective** compensation. The one who paid can then **claim back** from the others the share matching their responsibility.

> **⚖️ Worked example**
>
> A shop (controller) hires a cloud provider (processor). The provider ignores the contract and leaves a database unencrypted, and it leaks. The customer can claim the whole amount from either. If the shop pays, it can recover the provider's share, because the provider acted contrary to instructions.

## Sanctions: administrative fines

| Tier   | Maximum                                                                          | For breaches of                                                                                                          |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Higher | **€20,000,000 or 4%** of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher | Basic principles for processing and conditions for consent; data subjects' rights; rules on transfers to third countries |
| Lower  | **€10,000,000 or 2%**, whichever is higher                                    | Other infringements (e.g. organisational obligations)                                                                    |

An "undertaking" means a company or business group; the percentage applies to its worldwide turnover.

> **🧠 Memory hook**
>
> "**20/4 for the core, 10/2 for the rest.**" The core = principles and consent, rights, international transfers.

### Factors when setting a fine

- Nature, gravity and duration of the infringement; categories of data affected
- Intentional or negligent character
- Action taken to **mitigate** the damage to data subjects
- Degree of **cooperation** with the SA
- How the SA learned of it (self-reported by the entity, or complained by a data subject)

### Corrective powers of the SA

Issuing **orders**; **warnings and reprimands**; **temporary or permanent bans** on processing.

### EU institutions and bodies

Failures by EU officials or servants, intentional or negligent, make them liable to **disciplinary action** (Art. 49, as cited in the lecture).

> **⚠️ Exam traps**
>
> - "Whichever is **higher**", not lower.
>
> - Complaint to an SA: residence, work or infringement. Court claim: controller's establishment or residence. Do not mix the two lists.
>
> - Processors are not automatically liable; controllers are.
