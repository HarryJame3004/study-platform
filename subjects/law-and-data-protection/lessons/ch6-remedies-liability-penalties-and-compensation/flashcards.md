# Flashcards

## Card 1

Front:

Where to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority

Back:

Member State of habitual residence, place of work, or place of the alleged infringement.

---

## Card 2

Front:

Supervisory authority duties on complaints

Back:

Facilitate them (e.g. an electronic form), investigate, and inform the person of the outcome.

---

## Card 3

Front:

Who can appeal an SA decision to court?

Back:

Data subjects, controllers and processors.

---

## Card 4

Front:

Where to bring a court claim

Back:

Member State of the controller's or processor's establishment, or the data subject's habitual residence.

---

## Card 5

Front:

Court claim against a public authority exercising public powers

Back:

Only the courts of that authority's Member State.

---

## Card 6

Front:

Unlawful processing by EU institutions: which court?

Back:

The General Court of the EU (part of the CJEU).

---

## Card 7

Front:

Preliminary ruling: can the CJEU refuse?

Back:

It must answer; it cannot refuse for lack of relevance or timeliness. It can refuse only if the question is outside its competence.

---

## Card 8

Front:

Requirements for a not-for-profit body to represent people

Back:

Statutory objectives in the public interest, and active in data protection. Can bring collective claims.

---

## Card 9

Front:

Material damage

Back:

Out-of-pocket, objectively quantifiable loss, often secondary (job loss, contract denied, price discrimination).

---

## Card 10

Front:

Non-material damage

Back:

Emotional damage caused by the illegal processing itself.

---

## Card 11

Front:

When is a processor liable?

Back:

Only if it breached processor-specific obligations or acted outside or contrary to the controller's lawful instructions.

---

## Card 12

Front:

Several controllers or processors caused the damage

Back:

Each is liable for the entire damage, then can claim back the others' shares.

---

## Card 13

Front:

Higher fine tier and what it covers

Back:

€20m or 4% worldwide turnover, whichever is higher: principles and consent conditions, data subject rights, international transfers.

---

## Card 14

Front:

Lower fine tier

Back:

€10m or 2% worldwide turnover, whichever is higher: other infringements.

---

## Card 15

Front:

Factors when setting fines

Back:

Nature, gravity, duration, data categories; intent or negligence; mitigation; cooperation; how the SA learned.

---

## Card 16

Front:

Corrective powers of an SA

Back:

Orders; warnings and reprimands; temporary or permanent bans on processing.

---

## Card 17

Front:

Sanction for EU officials who fail their obligations

Back:

Disciplinary action.
