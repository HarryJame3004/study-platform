# Flashcards

## Card 1

Front:

Definition of international transfer

Back:

Transfer of personal data being processed or to be processed to a country outside the EEA or to an international organisation.

---

## Card 2

Front:

Which countries make up the EEA?

Back:

The EU member states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway.

---

## Card 3

Front:

The two-step test for transfers

Back:

1) The processing has a legal basis and complies with the GDPR; 2) the transfer complies with the international transfer rules.

---

## Card 4

Front:

Transfers within the EEA

Back:

Free. No EEA country may block transfers to another over protection concerns, if GDPR principles are respected.

---

## Card 5

Front:

Who decides adequacy?

Back:

The European Commission (for a country, territory, sector or international organisation).

---

## Card 6

Front:

What the Commission evaluates for adequacy

Back:

Rule of law; DP legal framework; independent DPA; international commitments; effective enforcement; data subject rights.

---

## Card 7

Front:

Adequate countries listed in the lecture

Back:

Andorra, Argentina, Canada (commercial), Faroe Islands, Guernsey, Israel, Isle of Man, Japan, Jersey, New Zealand, Switzerland, Uruguay, USA (framework).

---

## Card 8

Front:

EU-US Privacy Shield

Back:

Operational 1 August 2016; US companies self-certify and renew yearly; US Department of Commerce monitors. Replaced in July 2023 by the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

---

## Card 9

Front:

Appropriate safeguards (Art. 46)

Back:

SCCs; ad hoc clauses; binding corporate rules; codes of conduct; certification; public authority instruments.

---

## Card 10

Front:

The four SCC modules

Back:

Controller to controller; controller to processor; processor to processor; processor to controller.

---

## Card 11

Front:

SCC requirements

Back:

Transfer impact assessment; cannot modify core clauses; assess recipient country law; extra measures if needed; signed by both parties.

---

## Card 12

Front:

Ad hoc clauses: approval route

Back:

Submitted to the DPA (Art. 46(3)(a)); approved by the EDPB via the consistency mechanism (Art. 46(4)).

---

## Card 13

Front:

Binding corporate rules

Back:

Internal binding rules for multinational groups; must include principles, transparency, enforceability, rights, security, complaint procedures.

---

## Card 14

Front:

Codes of conduct for transfers require...

Back:

DPA approval, regular compliance checks, an accredited monitoring body.

---

## Card 15

Front:

Certification mechanisms

Back:

Third-party verification by accredited bodies, with criteria, audits, renewal, monitoring (e.g. EuroPriSe).

---

## Card 16

Front:

When may Art. 49 derogations be used?

Back:

Only when no adequacy decision and no appropriate safeguards are available; the controller must document why.

---

## Card 17

Front:

Derogations list

Back:

Explicit informed consent; contract with data subject; contract in their interest; important public interest; legal claims; vital interests; public register; compelling legitimate interests.

---

## Card 18

Front:

Conditions for the compelling legitimate interests derogation

Back:

No other derogation; not repetitive; limited number of people; not overridden; safeguards assessed; inform the SA; inform the data subject.

---

## Card 19

Front:

PNR agreements

Back:

EU agreements with the USA, Canada and Australia on passenger booking data, to fight serious crime and terrorism.

---

## Card 20

Front:

TFTP

Back:

EU-US Terrorist Finance Tracking Programme, covering financial data.
