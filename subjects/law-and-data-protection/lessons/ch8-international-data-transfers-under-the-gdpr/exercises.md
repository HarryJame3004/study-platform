# Exercises

## Chapter 8 MCQ

### Question 1

The EEA consists of:

- A. The EU only
- B. The EU plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway
- C. The EU plus Switzerland and the UK
- D. All Council of Europe members

Answer: B. The EU plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. Switzerland is not in the EEA (it has an adequacy decision).

---

### Question 2

Who adopts adequacy decisions?

- A. The EDPB
- B. National DPAs
- C. The European Commission
- D. The CJEU

Answer: C. The European Commission. The Commission decides adequacy.

---

### Question 3

In July 2023, the EU-US Privacy Shield was replaced by:

- A. Safe Harbor
- B. The EU-US Data Privacy Framework
- C. Binding corporate rules
- D. Convention 108+

Answer: B. The EU-US Data Privacy Framework. The DPF, administered by the ITA in the US Department of Commerce.

---

### Question 4

How many modules do the standard contractual clauses have?

- A. Two
- B. Three
- C. Four
- D. Six

Answer: C. Four. C2C, C2P, P2P, P2C.

---

### Question 5

Ad hoc contractual clauses must be:

- A. Used without approval
- B. Submitted to the DPA and approved by the EDPB via the consistency mechanism
- C. Approved by the US government
- D. Signed by the data subject

Answer: B. Submitted to the DPA and approved by the EDPB via the consistency mechanism. Art. 46(3)(a) and Art. 46(4).

---

### Question 6

Binding corporate rules are ideal for:

- A. Individuals transferring holiday photos
- B. Multinational groups with many intra-group transfers
- C. One-off transfers
- D. Public authorities only

Answer: B. Multinational groups with many intra-group transfers. Internal rules binding across the group.

---

### Question 7

Art. 49 derogations may be used:

- A. As the first choice
- B. Only when no adequacy decision and no appropriate safeguards are available
- C. Only for EEA transfers
- D. Instead of a lawful basis

Answer: B. Only when no adequacy decision and no appropriate safeguards are available. They are the last resort.

---

### Question 8

Which is NOT a condition for the 'compelling legitimate interests' derogation?

- A. The transfer is not repetitive
- B. Only a limited number of data subjects
- C. The SA and data subject are informed
- D. The transfer happens monthly for thousands of customers

Answer: D. The transfer happens monthly for thousands of customers. Repetitive, large-scale transfers cannot use it.

---

### Question 9

An Italian retailer sends a customer's name and address to a US courier to deliver an order. Which derogation fits?

- A. Explicit consent
- B. Performance of a contract with the data subject
- C. Public interest
- D. Public register

Answer: B. Performance of a contract with the data subject. Necessary to execute the sales contract the customer initiated.

---

### Question 10

A Spanish hospital sends records of an unconscious patient to a Japanese clinic for urgent treatment. Which derogation?

- A. Legal claims
- B. Vital interests
- C. Public register
- D. Contract

Answer: B. Vital interests. The patient cannot consent and life is at stake.

---

### Question 11

The EU has PNR agreements with:

- A. China, Russia, India
- B. The USA, Canada and Australia
- C. Japan and Korea
- D. Brazil and Argentina

Answer: B. The USA, Canada and Australia. Passenger Name Record agreements.

---

### Question 12

Under the two-step test, before applying transfer rules the controller must first:

- A. Get EDPB approval
- B. Ensure the processing has a legal basis and complies with the GDPR
- C. Appoint a DPO in the third country
- D. Anonymise the data

Answer: B. Ensure the processing has a legal basis and complies with the GDPR. Transfer conditions are additional to normal obligations.

---

### Question 13

Explicit consent as a transfer derogation requires that the person:

- A. Ticked a box in the terms
- B. Was informed of the possible risks due to no adequacy decision and no safeguards
- C. Is an EU citizen
- D. Signed before a notary

Answer: B. Was informed of the possible risks due to no adequacy decision and no safeguards. Informed of the risks, then explicitly consented.
