# Learning Objectives

Master Chapter 7 — Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) — well enough to answer both multiple-choice and written exam questions on it.

# Lesson

> **In one sentence:** A DPIA is a structured risk check you must do before starting processing that could seriously harm people: describe the processing, ask whether it is really necessary, list what could go wrong, reduce those risks, and write it all down.

## What is a DPIA?

A **systematic process** designed to:

- **Identify and evaluate risks** to the rights and freedoms of data subjects
- **Assess the necessity and proportionality** of processing
- **Implement measures to mitigate** the risks
- **Demonstrate accountability** and compliance

**Legal basis:** `Art. 35` is the main provision; detailed guidance in **WP248 rev.01** (endorsed by the EDPB). A DPIA implements **accountability** `Art. 5(2)` and **privacy by design** `Art. 25`.

## When is a DPIA required?

### Mandatory scenarios `Art. 35(3)`

- **Automated decision-making**: systematic, extensive evaluation through automated processing, including profiling, with legal or similarly significant effects.
- **Large-scale special categories**: Art. 9 data (health, biometric, genetic, ethnic, political, religious, sexual orientation) or Art. 10 criminal data.
- **Systematic public monitoring**: large-scale monitoring of publicly accessible areas (e.g. CCTV).

### The nine EDPB criteria: \*\*2 or more met means do a DPIA\*\*

| #   | Criterion                                                  | Examples                                                              |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Evaluation or scoring                                      | Credit scoring, behavioural profiling, health risk assessment         |
| 2   | Automated decision-making with legal or significant effect | Automated loan rejection, e-recruiting without human input            |
| 3   | Systematic monitoring                                      | Workplace monitoring, location tracking, internet surveillance        |
| 4   | Sensitive or highly personal data                          | Medical records, financial data, private communications, location     |
| 5   | Large-scale processing                                     | Consider geography, volume, duration, permanence                      |
| 6   | Matching or combining datasets                             | Data from several sources beyond reasonable expectations              |
| 7   | Vulnerable data subjects                                   | Children, employees, patients, elderly, asylum seekers, mental health |
| 8   | Innovative technology                                      | Facial recognition, fingerprints, IoT, AI and machine learning        |
| 9   | Prevents exercise of a right or use of a service           | Screening against fraud databases before providing a service          |

> **🧠 Memory hook for the nine criteria**
>
> "**E**very **A**utomated **S**ystem **S**hould **L**ook **M**ore **V**ery **I**ntensely **P**rotective": Evaluation, Automated decisions, Systematic monitoring, Sensitive data, Large scale, Matching datasets, Vulnerable subjects, Innovative tech, Prevents rights.

## The 8-step DPIA methodology

| Step                             | What you do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Screening                     | Decide if a DPIA is required. Ask: an Art. 35(3) scenario? 2 or more EDPB criteria? On the national SA's mandatory list? Similar processing already assessed? Output: a **documented screening decision with rationale**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 2. Description                   | Describe the processing `Art. 35(7)(a)`: **nature** (collect, store, use, delete), **scope** (categories, volume, geography, number of people), **context** (relationship, expectations, state of technology), **purpose**, **legal basis** (Art. 6 and Art. 9), **data flows** (diagram).                                                                                                                                                     |
| 3. Necessity and proportionality | `Art. 35(7)(b)` Check purpose limitation, data minimisation, storage limitation, accuracy, transparency, and mechanisms for data subject rights.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 4. Risk identification           | Impacts: **physical** (stalking, violence), **material** (financial loss, job loss, discrimination in services), **non-material** (reputation, distress, loss of confidentiality), **loss of rights** (loss of control, discrimination). Risk events: unauthorised access (**confidentiality**), unauthorised modification (**integrity**), loss of data (**availability**), **function creep** (expanding beyond the original purpose). |
| 5. Mitigation                    | **Technical** (encryption, pseudonymisation, anonymisation, access controls, firewalls, intrusion detection, secure coding, testing); **organisational** (policies, training, access management, incident response, vendor management); **legal/contractual** (Art. 28 processing agreements, standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules); **physical** (secure facilities, clean desk, secure disposal).                                 |
| 6. Consultation                  | **DPO** (mandatory where designated; document advice); IT and security teams; **data subjects** or representatives where appropriate `Art. 35(9)`; **prior consultation with the SA** if high risk **cannot be mitigated** `Art. 36`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 7. Documentation                 | Record description, necessity and risk assessment, measures and **residual risk**, DPO opinion, consultations, **final sign-off**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| 8. Implementation and review     | Put measures into operation; review when risk changes `Art. 35(11)`. Triggers: changes in operations, new technologies, security incidents.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

> **🧠 Memory hook for the 8 steps**
>
> "**S**ome **D**ogs **N**ever **R**un; **M**ost **C**ats **D**on't **R**ush": Screen, Describe, Necessity, Risks, Mitigate, Consult, Document, Review.

## DPIA quality checklist

A good DPIA is **systematic** (structured method), **comprehensive** (all aspects), **proportionate** (detail matches risk), **documented**, **consultative** (includes the DPO and stakeholders), **actionable** (concrete measures), **accountable** (clear responsibilities and sign-off), and **dynamic** (regularly reviewed).

> **⚠️ Exam traps**
>
> - The threshold is **two or more** criteria, not one.
>
> - A DPIA is done **before** the processing starts and then kept up to date; it is not a one-off.
>
> - Prior consultation with the SA (Art. 36) is only needed when **residual high risk cannot be mitigated**.
